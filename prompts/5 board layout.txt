Every pad is drawn at its true position, pin 1 as a black square, vias as
small grey dots, the silkscreen outlines in grey, the board edge in black.
Each IC is boxed and its pins numbered 1..N inside the body (outside would
collide with the neighbouring package - the chips sit 15 mm apart on a
7.5 mm body).  Designators are drawn upright at their real silkscreen
positions.  25 mm coordinate grid, letters across, digits down.

How the designators were read: the silkscreen is stroked vector text, so it
cannot be mirrored and stay readable.  The strokes were clustered into
words, the words into characters, and identical characters collapsed by an
exact quantised signature - 25 distinct glyphs on the lower board, 19 on the
upper.  Reading those 44 shapes once decodes every label.  Result: 172
designators on the lower board, 91 on the upper.

Two independent checks that it is right:

  - The upper board's decode is DD1-DD40, R1-R15, C1-C32, KT1-KT3, ZQ1 -
    exactly the parts list on sheet 3 of the factory schematic, nothing
    missing, nothing extra.
  - The pin counts found from pad geometry on the upper board (26 x DIP-16,
    9 x DIP-14, 3 x DIP-20, 2 x DIP-28) match that same list chip for chip.
  - The Gerber layout was compared against the factory drawing
    "Расположение радиоэлементов" on that sheet: element for element the
    same, so Mick's board is a faithful copy and the lower board's Gerber
    can be trusted the same way.

Footprints: 48 on the lower board (47 microcircuits + DA1), 40 on the upper

hard part: with chips on a 15 mm pitch and a 7.5 mm body, a pin-1 pad has a
neighbouring chip's row at 7.5 mm on BOTH sides, and the resistors mounted
in the gaps defeat the obvious tests.  What works is looking for the body
rectangle's two long sides - silkscreen segments inset 1.25 mm from each pad
row and running at least 80% of the package length.  Before that fix one
footprint straddled DD25 and DD24 and numbered neither correctly.

Orientation: the solder-side view is the board turned over about the

Checked, and they are right.  board-layout.pdf is now eight sheets - the
cover carries the verification and the two extra pages shift the drawings
to sheets 3-6, the indexes to 7-8.

The schematic gives a fingerprint that can be tested against copper: the
connector tables leave a row blank where a contact goes nowhere.  From the
GTL/GBL layers, for every connector pad, whether any trace ends on it (the
copper pours needed polarity handling - %LPD%/%LPC% - because P-CAD exports
a pour as a dark outline with a clear cut-out over it, and ignoring that
makes every pad look connected).

Upper board ПХЗ.099.006, where the power pins are routed on the board and
so nothing is missing - all nine rows match exactly:

    left connector    A={4,10}    B={3,7}   C={3,4,5,7}
    middle            A={}        B={7}     C={1,5,6,8}
    right             A={1}       B={}      C={2,4,5}

against the schematic's blank rows, and the row holding the square pad is A
in every case.  No other assignment of the letters produces those numbers -
for instance the middle connector's B is the only row with exactly contact
7 missing.

Lower board ПХЗ.099.001 gives the same sets plus its power contacts: +5V
and GND there run on the external bus bars (note 3 on sheet 3 of the
schematic), no trace reaches them on the board, so they land in the
"unrouted" list.  Subtract those and it matches.

Two independent confirmations, not from the blanks:

  - XS5 contacts С9 and С10 sit on one net, and that net reaches pin 8 of a
    DIP-16 - GND.  XS5.3 in the schematic says С9 = GND, С10 = GND.
  - XS4 contact В7 is a net of a single pad, no trace at all.  XS4.2 says
    В7 is empty.

Numbering direction was tested the same way: the sets match only when
counting from the square pad outward; reversed they do not.

One disagreement, now on the cover: contact В2 of XS3 (and the same contact
on the upper board's mating connector) IS routed - a three-pad net reaching
pin 12 of a DIP-14 - while the schematic's table leaves that row blank.
Same on both boards, so it looks like the table is incomplete rather than
the drawing being wrong.

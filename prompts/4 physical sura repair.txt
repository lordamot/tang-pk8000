What the symptoms already say, and what the document is built around

- The LED cycle at reset (three lit, then only "Вкл") is the ROM's first seven OUT
  instructions programming both ВВ55s: the РГ LED is DD33 PC5 (port 82h bit 5), the
  АЛФ LED is DD32 PC5 (port 86h bit 5), both through open-collector inverters in
  DD1. So the CPU is clocked, leaves reset, fetches ROM (DD41 and DD46), the data bus
  works both ways, the port decoder DD21 and the strobes in DD14 work.
- The next thing the ROM does is write 88h = 77h (a cyan border, grey on the
  monochrome composite) and test F000h-FFFFh, retrying for ever. Interrupts are
  enabled only after the test, and the keyboard is scanned only in the interrupt,
  so "no key reaction" is consistent with the machine standing in the RAM test. Your
  own note on this unit from July ("black screen with a grey border top and bottom")
  is exactly that picture.
- The beep at reset is a click from the piezo line changing level, not a ROM tone.
  Key click is off by default in this BASIC (the BEEPF flag at FA20h is zeroed by
  the RAM test), so "no beeps on keys" is not a symptom. The LEDs not reacting to
  ФИКС/АЛФ is.
- The DRAM, the clock generator, READY and the frame interrupt are all on the upper
  board, which works. What the lower board contributes to a RAM access is the
  address and data lines through XS4/XS5, the strobes WRRAM/ and RDR/, the bank
  multiplexer DD34 with its enable flip-flop DD5.1, the page decoder DD35 (CSRAM/),
  DD22 (SELROM/) and the READY line through XS3.1 A3. That is where the guide sends
  you, once a ten-minute multimeter test has confirmed the state.

How the document is ordered

1. Facts and what they imply (as above), with the three possible "black screens".
2. What sits on which board and the three inter-board connectors, signal by signal.
3. A multimeter-only table that tells apart "stuck in the RAM test", "booted to
   BASIC" and "not running": DD32 pin 39 (0 before the test, 1 after SCREEN 0), CPU
   INTE (pin 16) and INT (pin 14, permanently high when never acknowledged), DD5.1
   pin 6, the keyboard row lines KA0-KA3.
4. Stages 0-7: contacts and jumpers (CPU socket re-tinning fixed two of your other
   Suras; KT1-KT2 on both boards), supply rails including the on-board -5 V (R32,
   VD2), oscilloscope on Ф1/Ф2 (12 V swing at pins 22/15), reset, SYNC, READY/WAIT,
   HOLD; the oscilloscope signature of the stuck loop (WRRAM/ every 21-30 us, IOWR/
   never, only RDROM5/ active); the RAM path checks in order of likelihood; the video
   output stage (VT1, DD12.1, SI/, GI/); the "alive but no keyboard" branch; and only
   then chip replacement, with the EPROMs last.
5. Appendices: CPU and ВВ55 pins used, connector pin maps, the ROM's boot sequence
   with what each step shows, per-EPROM CRC32 and byte sums of BASIC 1.2 (every 2 KB
   chip sums to xx00h), and a measurement protocol to fill in.

One correction I made during review: the colour register outputs (DD30/DD31) sit on
a time-multiplexed bus and are enabled only during the border, so they cannot be
read statically with a multimeter; the guide uses DD32 pin 39 and INTE instead and
says to look at DD30/DD31 with a two-channel scope synchronised to SI/.

What this is and is not: pin numbers come from Mick's redrawn schematic of
ПХЗ.099.001/006 and the boot sequence from the disassembled ROM, checked against
the FPGA reimplementation's behaviour; nothing was measured on your board. The
"stuck in the RAM test" reading is the most consistent hypothesis, and section 3
is there to confirm or refute it before anything is desoldered.
